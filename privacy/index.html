Tidyboxes privacy policy

Tidyboxes: Desktop Organizer, by PaperKite. Effective 26 November 2026.

Tidyboxes doesn't collect any data, and your files never leave your PC. There's no account, no analytics or telemetry, and no ads. Tidyboxes goes online for only two things: the Microsoft Store, to sell and confirm Tidyboxes Pro, and, only if you sort photos by place or by trip, a free map service that turns a rough location into a place name.

Network use

Everything in Tidyboxes works offline except the two things below. It never sends your files, their names or contents, or anything about you or your desktop anywhere.

1. The Microsoft Store

The Microsoft Store's own purchase system is used to buy or restore Tidyboxes Pro and, now and then, to check whether your Windows account owns Pro. That check carries no information about you or how you use Tidyboxes. Microsoft handles purchases under the Microsoft Privacy Statement; Tidyboxes never sees your payment details. Once confirmed, Pro is remembered on your PC, so it keeps working offline.

2. Place names for photos (only if you choose it)

When you tidy a folder by place, by country or by trip or event, Tidyboxes reads the location your camera or phone saved inside your photos, on your PC. To name the place (say, "Lisbon, Portugal"), it sends a rough location, the middle of a group of photos rounded to about 1 km, to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service (nominatim.openstreetmap.org), run by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. Nothing else is sent: not your photos, their names or dates, nor anything about you. Each area of about 10 km is looked up once, and the name is remembered on your PC, so the same area is never asked about again.

As with any website, the service sees your internet address when Tidyboxes asks; its own privacy policy covers that. If you never sort by place or by trip, nothing is sent. Place names are © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database Licence.

What Tidyboxes looks at to work

To show and tidy your desktop, Tidyboxes reads the names, types, dates and icons of the files on your desktop, and where the icons sit. When you tidy another folder (Pro), it reads the same about the files in that folder and, depending on how you choose to sort: the date a photo was taken, where and with which camera; which website a download came from (from the note Windows keeps with each download); a song's artist and album; who wrote a document; the first page of a document's text, to tell an invoice from a payslip (scanned PDFs are read by Windows' own text recognition); the names of the apps installed on your PC, to spot installers you no longer need; and the contents of files, to spot exact copies. It uses all of this only on your PC, to do what you ask, and never shares or stores it, except the rough location described above when you sort by place or by trip.

What stays on your PC

Tidyboxes keeps a few small files of its own in %LocalAppData%\Tidyboxes:

  • your settings and shortcuts,
  • your list of boxes, your rules and your saved layouts,
  • the folders you've tidied and how you chose to sort them,
  • place names already looked up for photo locations, so each area is only asked about once,
  • a tidy history, so "Undo tidy" can put every file back where it was,
  • an encrypted licence record, so Tidyboxes Pro works offline,
  • a small safety record and a local log of errors, to help put things right after a crash.

They are never sent anywhere. Windows removes them when you uninstall Tidyboxes.

Your files

Tidyboxes never deletes or overwrites your files. A file in a box is moved into a plain folder inside your own Desktop folder: Desktop\Tidyboxes\<box name>. Your files are not stored anywhere else and are never uploaded. When you tidy another folder, like Downloads, its files are moved into folders inside that same folder, and "Undo" or "Put it back" returns them.

Microsoft Store apps can't run anything while they're being uninstalled, so your files stay in the Tidyboxes folder on your desktop after you uninstall. Nothing is lost. If you'd like your desktop exactly as it was, choose Settings > Tidy & rules > "Put everything back on the desktop" before uninstalling. A note in that folder ("What is this folder.txt") explains how to put things back by hand.

Children

Tidyboxes collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes and contact

If this policy ever changes, the new version will appear here with a new date. Questions are welcome: contact PaperKite via the support link on the Microsoft Store page for Tidyboxes.
